Terrain (elevation)
0 = off
bumps this small never block
Gaps in the primary source are filled first from coarser zoom levels of the same source, then from the fallback. Imported DTM files (below) override both inside their extent.
Why flat streets sometimes look blocked: free global elevation (Copernicus GLO-30, EU-DEM, SRTM) is a 25–30 m grid with ±2–5 m error, and in towns it partly includes rooftops. From eye height a 2 m bump 50 m away hides everything behind it. Smoothing removes the grid noise; the tolerance lets terrain bumps smaller than this value never block the view (buildings, trees and your imported DTM/DSM are never relaxed). Raise it in dense cities, set both to 0 with a LiDAR DTM.

Sources & limits
Buildings: OpenStreetMap via Overpass. Heights come from height, then building:levels × storey height (+ roof), then Overture's height for the same footprint, and finally the default. Overture Maps (monthly releases, read from its catalog) adds ML footprints where OSM has none. Click any building with Inspect to see where its height came from, or correct it.
Terrain: Mapterhorn is Copernicus GLO-30 worldwide, plus national LiDAR terrain models at up to 0.5 m where countries publish them openly. AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen) is SRTM / EU-DEM blend, around 30 m in Europe. Copernicus and EU-DEM are surface-ish models smoothed at 30 m: in cities they can carry some building and tree height. Use a LiDAR DTM where you can.
Imagery: CARTO, OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap, CyclOSM, HOT, Esri World Imagery (current + Clarity + Wayback archive since 2014), Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX (yearly, 10 m), USGS (US only), plus Google, Mapbox, MapTiler and Stadia with your own keys, and any XYZ/WMS you add.
Math: rectilinear pinhole projection with heading, tilt and roll. The exact frame boundary is computed per azimuth. Earth curvature and standard refraction (k = 0.13) are applied. Sun position uses the NOAA algorithm with atmospheric refraction. DoF uses thin-lens hyperfocal formulas.
  • The scene is a 2.5D height surface: no arches, passages or bridges to see under, and no balconies, signs, cars or walls. A drone or LiDAR DSM import captures most of this.
  • Roofs are flat at the given height (gables reach it only at the ridge).
  • Fisheye and lens distortion are not modelled. Real lenses breathe differently from the thin-lens model.
  • Vertical datums differ between sources (e.g. Hungarian EOMA vs EGM2008 can differ by tens of cm). Use the offset field when mixing an imported DTM/DSM with other terrain.
  • Sensor dimensions: verify critical modes against the manufacturer's frame-line / format tools.
